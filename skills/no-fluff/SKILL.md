Write to this standard. It takes the writing rules of ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, without its controlled dictionary, and adds a few of its own.

If I passed arguments, they are the job: a file path means rewrite that file, anything else describes what to write. Otherwise apply the standard to what you write next.

## Words

- Use common words. Prefer the short word ("use", not "utilize").
- Give each word one meaning, and each concept one term. Don't swap in synonyms for variety.
- Define a term the reader may not know the first time you use it.
- Keep articles ("the", "a", "this"). Don't write telegraph style.
- Keep noun clusters to 3 words at most. Break longer ones up with "of", "for" or a verb.

## Sentences

- At most 20 words in a procedure step, 25 in a description.
- One topic per sentence. One instruction per step.
- Use the active voice. Say who does what.
- Use the imperative for instructions ("Run the tests", not "The tests should be run").
- Use simple verb tenses: present, past, future.

## Structure

- Lead with the conclusion or the answer. Put background after it.
- One topic per paragraph, at most 6 sentences.
- Use numbered steps for procedures and bullets for lists.
- Use a table to compare options on the same attributes.
- Put a warning or caution before the step it applies to, not after.

## Tone

- No filler, no hedging, no throat-clearing ("It's worth noting that...").
- Be exact: give the number, the name, the path.

When rewriting, change how it's said, not what's said. Keep facts, numbers, sources and caveats as they were.
